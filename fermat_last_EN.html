Beal Conjecture ($1M AMS prize)i The form A⁽+Bᴯ=Cᵣ with differing exponents — still unsolved
First posed in 1993 by Texas banker Andy Beal. The AMS raised the prize to $1 million in 2013, and still holds it today. Try it yourself in the "Beal Conjecture" tab on the left.
Generalized Fermat curves & the Hasse principlei The form Axⁿ+Byⁿ+Czⁿ=0 with varied coefficients, and counterexamples to the local-global principle
Selmer's curve 3x³+4y³+5z³=0 is a famous counterexample (violating the Hasse principle) — it appears to have solutions over every prime p and over the reals, but has none over the rationals.
Extension to real quadratic fields (Freitas–Siksek)i Ongoing research into whether FLT holds over number fields beyond the rationals
Freitas–Siksek (2014) proved FLT over Q(√d) for 3≤d≤23 (d≠5,17); Michaud-Jacobs (2021/22) extended it to many values of d in 26≤d≤97.
LMFDBi The elliptic-curve and modular-form database at the heart of Wiles's proof
A worldwide collaborative database containing roughly 24 million L-functions, 850,000 modular forms, 7.5 million varieties, and 21 million number fields.
Quanta — What is the Langlands Program?i The big picture from Frey curves to the Modularity Theorem
A network of conjectures linking number theory to harmonic analysis/geometry, often called the "grand unified theory of mathematics." In 2024 the geometric Langlands conjecture was proved after 30 years.
Faltings's Theorem (the Mordell Conjecture)i Curves of genus 2 or higher have only finitely many rational points — see the Complex Surface mode below
Conjectured by Mordell in 1922, proved by Faltings in 1983. It only guarantees "finite" — showing "zero" requires stronger tools, like Wiles's modularity proof.

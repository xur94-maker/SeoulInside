The Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer product

For each good prime p, count Nₚ points mod p (exactly, via a square-root table) and multiply Nₚ/p. Conjecturally the product behaves like C·(log X)ʳ.

This is an observed trend, not a theorem: convergence is slow and noisy, so the slope of this graph is not a reliable rank estimator (you can see 37a1 hardly grows). The rigorous detector of rank is the L-function in tab 2. The check "max |aₚ|/(2√p) ≤ 1" is the Hasse bound, verified for every prime shown.

The completed L-function Λ(E,s)

The truncated Euler product does not converge at s = 1, so it cannot be used there. Instead we use modularity: Λ(s) = Σ aₙ[(√N/2πn)ˢ Γ(s,2πn/√N) + ε(√N/2πn)²⁻ˢ Γ(2−s,2πn/√N)] converges everywhere and satisfies Λ(s) = ε·Λ(2−s). Its zero order at s = 1 is the analytic rank.

The an come from point counts (ap = p+1−Nₚ) and the Hecke recursion; compare them with the q-expansion on LMFDB. "Numerically zero" means below 10⁻⁹, which is strong evidence, not a proof. Root numbers ε = aN hold for prime conductor.

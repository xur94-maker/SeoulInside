About this tab

This page lets you walk the Rules A, B, and C of the final article and the central gravity of Section 9 yourself. The theory curves are exact results; whether the number of steps is physical time depends on the rule. Whenever you change a control, 4,000 sailors start walking again from the beginning.

Basis and sources

Exponential stationary distribution at a reflecting floor: Harrison (1985); sedimentation equilibrium is the Laplace–Perrin law that Perrin confirmed. The detailed balance of Rules A and B and the tilt y = g/2 of B are the construction of Section 2 of the article. The scale height of surface air (288 K) is 8.43 km, and the height at which the density falls tenfold is 19.42 km.

Absorbing floor: inverse Gaussian first-passage time (Schrödinger, Smoluchowski 1915). The discrete-time correction 0.5826 is the value of Broadie–Glasserman–Kou (1997) and holds only for Gaussian increments; this mode uses Gaussian increments, so it applies. For the uniform spherical walk it is 0.516σ (0.298).

Return: the g = 0 limit 1 − 1/(2u − 1) = 0.50806 (u = 1.51639) follows from the half-space folding correspondence (the derivation of Section 8.5 of the article). The recurrence for g > 0 follows from the conditional-independence decomposition in discrete time (Section 8.2); since pn(0) goes to 0, the return probability is 1 but the mean return time is infinite, that is, null recurrence.

Central gravity (Section 9 of the article): only Rule A (Metropolis) has exact detailed balance, so this tab uses Rule A. For the constant attraction U = g·r, the radial distribution is the gamma distribution (g³/2)r²e−gr (mean 3h, mode 2h), and for the spring attraction U = b·r²/2 it is the Maxwell distribution. By Kac's lemma (E[T] = 1/π(0) for a positive recurrent chain), the mean return time on the lattice tab is Z = Σ e−g|x| (Section 9.4), and the spectral interpretation of the relaxation time (hydrogen-atom levels) is a result in the diffusive limit (Section 9.5).

Newtonian tab (Section 9.6 of the article): numerical integration of the shell weight r²e−Λ(1−R/r) on [R, X·R] with an isothermal Boltzmann factor. Λ = 755 for Earth (R = 6371 km, scale height 8.43 km). The Moon values 34, 39, and 25 are the article's own calculations from NASA data for 15 C air, 250 K, and 390 K. This is a closed-region yardstick under a constant-temperature assumption and does not replace thermal-escape (Jeans) physics.

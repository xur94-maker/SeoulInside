# The Trickle-Dry Effect

**TL;DR:** "Trickle-down" is debated; "trickle-dry" is observed everywhere but never named. When an anchor employer fails or leaves – a factory, shipyard, or mine – damage is immediate, asymmetric, and often irreversible. Restaurants close, property values crash, schools shrink, and original residents leave. If the company later recovers, recovery goes to newcomers, not those who held on. Cases from Gunsan (GM closure), Geoje (record profits with disconnected foreign labor), and Gangwon coal towns show the same cruel pattern.

# The Legibility Paradox

**TL;DR:** Between 1980 and 2026, the Korean won lost 40x domestic purchasing power, the dollar lost 4x, yet the exchange rate moved only 1.8x – because all major currencies fell together. This is the Rubber Ruler Problem: when every measuring stick shrinks, you cannot see the change. Korea's dramatic transformation (GDP per capita from $1,700 to $35,000) made its currency legible while stable currencies hid their debasement. The Legibility Paradox: the currency cited as proof of monetary instability is the only one honest enough to bend visibly. Gangnam's rise tracked real growth, not speculation.

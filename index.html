Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer (BSD) Conjecture (Prologue) Post: The Equation That Counts What Cannot Be Counted Link: https://seoulinside.substack.com/p/the-equation-that-counts-what-cannot Published: 2026-06-01 | Type: Millennium Problem Prologue This prologue introduces the BSD conjecture intuitively, without heavy mathematical formalism. The author starts with a simple curve, y² = x³ − x, and asks: how many points on this curve have rational coordinates? The answer is infinitely many. But y² = x³ + 1 has exactly two rational points, and y² = x³ − 43x + 166 has exactly one. No obvious pattern emerges. In 1960 at Cambridge, Bryan Birch and Peter Swinnerton-Dyer used EDSAC, one of the world’s first electronic computers, to count points on elliptic curves modulo primes p (N_p). They computed the product ∏ N_p/p and observed that curves where the product grew slowly (like log X) had infinitely many rational points, while curves where the product stabilized had only finitely many. The conjecture states that the order of vanishing (analytic rank) of the L-function L(E,s) = ∏ (1 - a_p p^{-s} + p^{1-2s})^{-1} at s=1 equals the number of independent rational points (algebraic rank) on the curve. This connects geometry (rational points) to analysis (L-function zeros). Partial results include Coates-Wiles (1977, rank 0), Gross-Zagier (1986, rank 1), and Kolyvagin (1989-90, rank 0 and 1 cases, also proving finiteness of the Tate-Shafarevich group Ш for these ranks). For rank ≥ 2, almost nothing is proven. The full BSD formula includes Ш, the mysterious Tate-Shafarevich group, which measures the failure of the local-global principle (solutions everywhere locally but no global rational solution). 3. Lowering viscosity (increasing Reynolds number) and raising amplitude creates an energy cascade from large to small scales, with the question being whether this cascade can overcome viscous damping. The core difference between 2D and 3D lies in the vortex stretching term (ω·∇)u. In 2D, this term vanishes identically, making the equations globally solvable. In 3D, vortex stretching can amplify vorticity, potentially leading to blow-up. Known results: Leray (1934) proved global existence of weak solutions in 3D, but these solutions may have singularities and are not known to be unique. Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg (1982) proved that any singularities must occur on a set of parabolic Hausdorff dimension at most 1 (i.e., they cannot fill a region, curve, or surface). Tao (2016) proved finite-time blow-up for an averaged version of the Navier-Stokes equations, but this system suppresses small-scale interactions present in the real equations. The simulation offers four presets: Lamb-Oseen (classical exact solution, decays smoothly), Taylor-Green (turbulence benchmark, smoothness believed but unproven), Leray 1934 (near the boundary of weak solution theory), and Tao Averaged (approximates the 2016 construction). The official problem statement by Charles Fefferman (Princeton) asks: either prove that for any smooth initial condition in 3D, a smooth global solution exists, or find a smooth initial condition that produces a finite-time blow-up. As of May 2026, the prize remains unclaimed. 7. Navier-Stokes Equations (Technical Reference) Post: Navier-Stokes Equations — An Archive Link: https://seoulinside.substack.com/p/navierstokes-equations-an-archive Published: 2026-05-31 | Type: Multi-Notation Technical Reference This document is a comprehensive technical reference on the Navier-Stokes Existence and Smoothness problem, with careful clarification of the Millennium Problem’s precise scope. The spatial domain is either ℝ³ (decay at infinity: u → 0 as |x| → ∞) or 𝕋³ (periodic boundary conditions). Bounded domains with physical walls (no-slip condition) are explicitly excluded from the problem statement. This is not a formatting detail—the presence of boundaries fundamentally changes the mathematics, creating boundary layers, corner singularities, and making the vanishing-viscosity limit singular. The Millennium Problem concerns only the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations: ∇·u = 0, ρ = constant. Compressible fluid material included in the document (shock waves, Rankine-Hugoniot conditions) is for physical completeness and is not directly relevant to the prize question. The document provides precise interpretations of three major theorems: Beale-Kato-Majda criterion (1984): If blow-up occurs at time T, then ∫₀^{T} ‖ω(t)‖_L∞ dt diverges. The common misreading—”blow-up occurs when vorticity becomes infinite”—is incorrect. If ‖ω‖_L∞ ~ 1/√(T*-t), the integral converges, and BKM makes no conclusion. Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg (1982): The singular set of any suitable weak solution has parabolic Hausdorff dimension at most 1, and P¹(S)=0. This rules out singularities forming along curves, surfaces, or volumes, but does NOT rule out isolated point singularities or fractal sets with dimension < 1. Tao (2016): Finite-time blow-up for an averaged system where the advecting velocity is replaced by a spatially smoothed version (u ∗ φ). The averaging removes small-scale back-reactions present in the true equations. Many experts believe these interactions might be precisely what prevents blow-up. The nonlinear term (u·∇)u is quadratic (doubling u quadruples the output) and self-referential. The error amplification equation ∂ₜw + (u·∇)w + (w·∇)v = νΔw shows how the term (w·∇)v amplifies errors. Yet the energy identity ∫ (u·∇)u·u dV = 0 shows the nonlinear term redistributes energy but does not create or destroy it. Leray-Hopf weak solutions (1934) satisfy a global energy inequality (≤ instead of =), permitting “phantom dissipation” at singular points. Uniqueness is known to fail (Albritton, Brué, Colombo, 2022). Suitable weak solutions (local energy inequality) restrict singularities further. Smooth classical solutions are what the Millennium Problem asks for. The document includes physical grounding: Reynolds number regimes (Stokes flow at Re≪1 is time-reversible; golf ball dimples trigger drag crisis at Re~2×10⁵, reducing drag coefficient from 0.47 to 0.09), Kolmogorov scale η = (ν³/ε)^{1/4} (laboratory water tank: ~18 μm, atmospheric boundary layer: ~0.23 mm), and DNS cost scaling Re³ (Re=10⁷ requires ~10²¹ operations, structurally infeasible). 8. Instead, Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) with turbulence modeling is used. The Riemann zeta function is defined for Re(s)>1 by ζ(s) = Σ_{n=1}^∞ 1/n^s, with the Euler product ζ(s) = ∏_p (1-p^{-s})^{-1} providing the fundamental connection to primes. Analytic continuation to Re(s)>0 is achieved via Euler-Maclaurin summation: ζ(s) = 1/(s-1) + 1/2 + s∫₁^∞ ({x}-1/2)/x^{s+1} dx. A more powerful approach uses the Jacobi theta function θ(t) = Σ_{n=-∞}^∞ e^{-πn²t} and Poisson summation to derive π^{-s/2}Γ(s/2)ζ(s) = (1/2)∫₀^∞ t^{s/2-1}(θ(t)-1)dt. The functional equation is ζ(s) = 2^s π^{s-1} sin(πs/2) Γ(1-s) ζ(1-s), or symmetrically ξ(s)=ξ(1-s) where ξ(s)=(1/2)s(s-1)π^{-s/2}Γ(s/2)ζ(s). Trivial zeros occur at s = -2, -4, -6,... The von Mangoldt function Λ(n) (log p if n=p^k, 0 otherwise) appears in the logarithmic derivative: ζ’(s)/ζ(s) = -Σ Λ(n)/n^s. The explicit formula connects primes to zeros: ψ(x) = x - Σ_ρ x^ρ/ρ - log(2π) - (1/2)log(1-x^{-2}), where ψ(x) = Σ_{p^k ≤ x} log p. Partial results via the Levinson method: Levinson (1974) proved at least 34.74% of zeros lie on the critical line; Conrey (1989) raised this to 40.88%; Pratt, Robles, Zaharescu, Zeindler (2020) achieved 41.72% (5/12). The Guth-Maynard breakthrough (2024) improved zero-density estimates for the first time since Ingham (1940), proving bounds of the form N(σ,T) ≤ C·T^{A(1-σ)}. This is not a proof of RH but a significant analytic advance. The Montgomery-Odlyzko Law connects ζ zeros to random matrix theory (GUE): the pair correlation function R_2(y) = 1 - (sin(πy)/(πy))². This was proven for function field zeta functions by Katz and Sarnak (1999). In 1859, Bernhard Riemann wrote an 8-page paper that found structure underlying the seemingly random distribution of prime numbers, but he could not prove his central claim: all non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function have real part 1/2. If true, RH provides precise tools for understanding prime distribution and validates approximately 10,000 theorems proven conditionally on RH. If false, those theorems collapse. The stakes extend to cryptography, primality testing, and the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH), which is even more practically significant. Partial progress timeline: Hardy (1914) proved infinitely many zeros lie on the critical line. Levinson (1974) proved at least 34.74% are on the line. Conrey (1989) raised this to 40.88%. The current record (Pratt, Robles, Zaharescu, Zeindler, 2020) is 41.72% (5/12). Computational verification has extended to approximately 10 trillion (10¹³) zeros, all found on the critical line—but this does not constitute a proof. The 2018 Atiyah affair: At age 89, Fields Medal and Abel Prize winner Michael Atiyah announced a 15-line proof at Heidelberg. The core object, the “Todd function,” was not rigorously defined, and the proof made almost no use of the zeta function’s properties. Mathematician Peter Woit, invoking physicist Wolfgang Pauli’s phrase, called it “not even wrong”—meaning so fundamentally flawed it cannot be meaningfully evaluated. Atiyah died four months later. The episode demonstrates that status does not substitute for proof. The 2024 Guth-Maynard breakthrough improved zero-density estimates for the first time in 84 years, but Maynard himself noted: “A complete proof would require entirely new ideas that don’t yet exist.” The 2025-2026 preprint surge (Nielsen & Semita, Kuipers, Moon with AI agents, Jenssen) has produced multiple claims, but none have survived peer review. The Montgomery-Odlyzko Law emerged from a 1972 tea-time encounter at Princeton’s Institute for Advanced Study. Hugh Montgomery showed his pair correlation formula to physicist Freeman Dyson, who recognized it as the eigenvalue statistics of the Gaussian Unitary Ensemble (GUE) from nuclear physics. Andrew Odlyzko’s computational verification at Bell Labs showed stunning agreement. The Hilbert-Polya conjecture suggests zeros might be eigenvalues of an undiscovered physical operator. 11. P vs NP (Technical Report) Post: P vs. NP — The Open Problem Link: https://seoulinside.substack.com/p/p-vs-np-the-open-problem Published: 2026-05-31 | Type: Computational Complexity Technical Report This document is a technical report on the P vs. NP problem, organized around formal definitions, barrier analysis, proof construction, exercises, and resource mapping. P is the class of decision problems solvable by a deterministic Turing machine in polynomial time (O(n^k)). NP is the class where a candidate solution can be verified in polynomial time. The three barriers: Relativization (Baker-Gill-Solovay, 1975): There exist oracles A and B such that P^A = NP^A and P^B ≠ NP^B. Any proof that works relative to all oracles (relativizing proof) cannot resolve P vs. NP. Diagonalization arguments relativize. Natural Proofs (Razborov-Rudich, 1997): A natural property (constructive + large + useful against P/poly) would break pseudorandom generators. Since secure PRGs (RSA, AES) are believed to exist, natural proofs likely do not exist. Razborov proved his own approximation method cannot extend to P vs. NP. Algebrization (Aaronson-Wigderson, 2009): Extends relativization to algebraic oracles, closing off arithmetization-based techniques (IP=PSPACE, etc.). Yannakakis (1988) proved that expressing Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP) with a symmetric linear program requires exponentially many constraints—the only result in Woeginger’s archive that has been peer-reviewed and accepted. Recent developments: Kabanets & Kolokolova (ECCC 2025/089, revised March 2026) connect time-bounded Kolmogorov complexity chain rules to P vs. NP, conditional on cryptographic hardness assumptions. Meta-complexity (Oxford, 2025-2026) treats the difficulty of proofs as a complexity question. Geometric Complexity Theory (Mulmuley & Sohoni) encodes P vs. NP in representation theory: if the permanent (representing #P-hardness) cannot be expressed as a determinant of polynomial-size matrix, then P ≠ NP. 2024-2026 proof attempts: Lee (Jan 2026, P=NP, failed on specific 3-SAT instances), Bhattacharjee (May 2026, P≠NP, Fourier-entropy approach, unreviewed), Goertzel (2025, P≠NP, non-relativizing approach targeting algebrization), among others. None have survived scrutiny. 12.

What this demonstrates

The verifier checks the answer in O(n) time — one pass through the subset. The solver, without a hint, must check up to 2ⁿ subsets. For n = 20, that's over a million checks. For n = 60, more than a billion billion.

P vs NP asks: is there a smarter way to find the answer — one that's as fast as checking it? No one has proved yes. No one has proved no.

Why this matters

The Deolalikar episode shows two things simultaneously: the mathematical community's capacity for rapid, distributed verification — and the structural reason why every proof attempt fails at the same points.

All four flaws map directly onto the three formal barriers: Relativization (1975), Natural Proofs (1997), Algebrization (2009). Deolalikar's proof didn't fail by accident. It failed at the exact locations the barriers predicted it would.

EXPERIMENT C Run Any Proof Strategy. Hit a Barrier.

Every proof attempt in history has been blocked by at least one of three formal barriers. Choose a proof strategy below and watch it collide with the barrier structure. No known technique survives all three simultaneously.

Barrier Year Status Why

The three barriers

Relativization (1975) — Baker, Gill, Solovay proved that diagonalization cannot resolve P vs NP. Any proof that works relative to an arbitrary oracle cannot settle the question.

Natural Proofs (1997) — Razborov & Rudich proved that any "large and constructive" circuit lower bound proof would break modern cryptography. Since cryptography works, such proofs cannot exist.

Algebrization (2009) — Aaronson & Wigderson proved that arithmetization techniques — the method behind IP = PSPACE — also cannot resolve P vs NP.

A valid proof must be: non-relativizing AND non-naturalizing AND non-algebrizing.

As of 2026, no known technique satisfies all three. This is why the problem is still open.

Context

The universe is approximately 4.3 × 10¹⁷ seconds old. A year is about 3.15 × 10⁷ seconds. When the table says "longer than the age of the universe" — that is not a metaphor. It is a calculation.

This is why P vs NP matters for cryptography: if factoring a 2048-bit RSA key takes 10¹⁰⁰ operations, no computer ever built can break it — regardless of speed. The hardness is mathematical, not technological.

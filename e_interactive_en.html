How does this work?

The most practical series for computing e is the Taylor series.

e = Σ[k=0,∞] 1/k! = 1 + 1 + 1/2! + 1/3! + ⋯

Summing the terms one by one would need a huge division at every step, so the terms are instead combined recursively in halves — a technique called binary splitting. All arithmetic runs as arbitrary-precision integers via JavaScript's BigInt. This is the same background technique behind the actual world record for e (December 2023, y-cruncher, 35 trillion digits). Unlike π's Chudnovsky algorithm, e's series needs no square roots, so it is pure integer arithmetic and comparatively simpler.

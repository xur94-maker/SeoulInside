Missile Pursuit Experiment

The discrete pursuit model from section 2-3, plus inertia α. Steer the hero with the arrow keys, or run the automatic simulation to compare the prediction weight w and the inertia α.

Each key press is one detection: the hero moves u, then the missile moves by the rule.

α=1 means no inertia; the smaller α is, the more slowly the direction changes.

Distances are |Pn − Mn| at the detection moment (before the missile moves). The inertia model blends the target movement D with the previous velocity V: Vn+1 = (1−α)Vn + αD, Mn+1 = Mn + Vn+1. At launch the missile is assumed to be already flying toward the hero at speed s. In Play mode you are caught when the distance at a detection is at most R, and you survive if you last N steps (a missile passing close between detections is not checked). "Keeps turning" follows the zig rule of the document's code E-11. This is a simplified model for learning recurrences and differs from real games and guidance systems. With inertia, the formula "remaining gap = (1−w)·u" no longer holds, so read the results as simulations only.

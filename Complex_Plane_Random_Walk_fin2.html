The Drunken Sailor in the Complex Plane

Feynman's own version of the 2D random walk: instead of stepping north, south, east, or west, each step points in a uniformly random direction between 0° and 360°. Represent a step as a unit complex number and the whole walk falls out of one line of algebra.

z = e^(iθ), θ ~ Uniform(0, 2π)  —  position after n steps: Z_n = ∑ e^(iθk)

Ready. Press "Run trials" to watch 20,000 walkers take 1,000 random-angle steps over roughly 20 seconds — try pushing the sliders to 80,000 particles and 10,000 steps.

Why this is the cleaner setup than a 4-direction grid. On a north/south/east/west lattice, every step spends itself entirely on one axis, so the number of horizontal versus vertical steps is itself random. Here, every single step contributes to both axes at once via cosθ and sinθ, so Var(Re Zn) and Var(Im Zn) come out to exactly n/2 each, and Cov = 0 falls out of ∫cosθsinθ dθ = 0 over a full circle — no conditioning arguments required.

GPS: if every walker were a GPS fix scattered around the true position by independent, equal-variance north/south and east/west errors, the median R above is exactly the receiver's quoted CEP — the radius that half your fixes fall inside. Speckle: R is the light's amplitude; what a sensor or your eye actually measures is intensity, R², which is why speckle brightness follows an Exponential curve even though the underlying field is Rayleigh. Fading: a mobile receiver sees signal envelope R fluctuate as it moves through a multipath field; the fraction of walkers landing well below the mean R is exactly the receiver's outage probability at that fade depth.
